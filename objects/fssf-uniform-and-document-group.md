# Adolph Osmera

**First Special Service Force | World War II**

### Military Service

- Adolph Osmera served with Headquarters Company, 1st Regiment, First Special Service Force (FSSF), an elite joint American-Canadian military formation established in 1942.
- The Force trained at Fort William Henry Harrison near Helena, Montana, where Osmera was photographed during his early service.
- In September 1942, Osmera received a temporary appointment to corporal, documented by an original certificate signed by Colonel Robert T. Frederick, the Force's commanding officer.
- In 1943, Osmera deployed with the FSSF to the Aleutian Islands, where the Force participated in operations associated with the recapture of Kiska.
- Among his surviving possessions is a hand-carved wooden object inscribed "Amchitka 1943."
- Following the Aleutian campaign, the FSSF deployed to Italy, participating in combat operations against German forces.
- Osmera served as a scout attached to Headquarters Company during the Italian campaign.
- The Force participated in the fighting around Monte la Difensa and subsequently at Anzio, where it conducted patrols, raids, and other operations against German positions.
- A surviving wartime diary, written by another member of the Force and retained among Osmera's personal effects, refers to him by the nickname "Ozzie."
- **April 15, 1944:** The diary records, "Ozzie got hit this morning." The entry appears amid descriptions of combat activity near Anzio. The precise circumstances of the incident are not established.
- **May 18, 1944:** The same diarist records, "Ozzie went to hospital with his back. Will be gone 6 weeks and maybe re-classified. All by myself now."
- **July 20, 1944:** Special Orders No. 168, issued by Headquarters, 3rd General Hospital, identify Osmera as a Technician Fifth Grade assigned to the 1st Regiment, FSSF. The orders document his relief from attachment to the Airborne Training Center and assignment to the Detachment of Patients, 37th General Hospital, for temporary duty with the Motor Transport Section.
- Osmera's surviving wartime diaries and personal documentation largely end with the Anzio campaign. Following his wounds and subsequent hospitalization in May 1944, he appears not to have returned to active combat duty with the First Special Service Force, missing its later operations in Southern France.
- His surviving four-pocket service blouse retains corporal chevrons and an EAME Campaign Medal ribbon with two bronze service stars. However, Department of the Army correspondence dated June 26, 1953, confirms his entitlement to three bronze service stars. These discrepancies suggest that the uniform was not updated to reflect his later designation as Technician Fifth Grade or his complete campaign entitlements.
- Osmera subsequently returned to the United States, where surviving records document his presence at the Army Air Forces Convalescent Hospital at Camp Carson, Colorado, in March 1945.

### Personal Effects and Documentation

- **Original uniform:** Osmera's named service blouse, trousers, web belt, Sam Browne belt, and cap, preserved together.
- **Military paperwork:** Original orders, certificates, correspondence, and administrative documents relating to his military service, including the September 1942 appointment signed by Colonel Robert T. Frederick.
- **Wartime photographs:** Photographs documenting Osmera's service in Montana, the Aleutian Islands, and Italy, including images personally identified or annotated by Osmera.
- **Wartime diary:** A surviving copy of another FSSF soldier's diary, containing contemporary references to Osmera and the Force's experiences during the Italian campaign.
- **Hand-carved wooden object:** A souvenir inscribed "Amchitka 1943," personally made by Osmera according to his widow, Regina.
- **Handmade letter opener:** A letter opener incorporating two FSSF "fat tail" shoulder patches into its handle, also made by Osmera according to Regina.
- **FSSF history:** Osmera's personal first-edition (1947) copy of Robert D. Burhans's *The First Special Service Force: A War History of the North Americans, 1942–1944*, containing his handwritten annotations, including the names of fallen comrades he circled and a photograph in which he identified himself.
- **First edition of *Anzio*:** An inscribed copy retained among his personal possessions.
- **Shorthand notes:** Numerous handwritten shorthand pages retained by Osmera. Regina recalled his use of shorthand to prevent Germans from reading his notes if they were captured.
- **Wartime publications:** Period military newsletters and printed materials, including issues of the *Braves Bulletin* associated with the First Special Service Force.
- **Personal ephemera:** Additional correspondence, religious objects, and miscellaneous wartime possessions.

### Provenance

- Osmera's uniform, consisting of his named blouse, Insignia, trousers, web belt, Sam Browne belt, and cap, was originally sold by a family member to a collector and personal friend at a local gun show.
- **In 2006**, the collector sold the uniform to the author in the same condition in which he had acquired it.
- Using the name found inside the uniform, the author located Osmera through a telephone directory and subsequently contacted his widow, Regina Osmera.
- **In 2006**, the author met with Regina and acquired additional material, including loose insignia, wartime photographs, military paperwork, and personal ephemera.
- Regina also possessed an extensive scrapbook containing photographs, handwritten captions, documents, and ephemera documenting Osmera's military service. She wished to retain the scrapbook within the family but permitted its contents to be scanned.
- These acquisitions reunited Osmera's named uniform with a substantial collection of his original wartime photographs, documents, and personal effects.
