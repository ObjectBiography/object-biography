## The Uniforms

* Two original three-color Desert Camouflage Uniform (DCU) blouses belonging to Joe Munoz, a U.S. Army Special Forces warrant officer.
* Both worn during his 2005 deployment to Iraq with Operational Detachment Alpha (ODA) 774, 7th Special Forces Group (Airborne).
* Both named to MUNOZ and retaining their original warrant officer rank and unit insignia.
* Garrison DCU: Fully badged example worn for official meetings and engagements with senior military personnel.
* FOB DCU: More heavily worn example retaining identical rank and unit insignia, but without the sewn qualification badges.
* The fully badged blouse is documented in two period photographs taken in Iraq and supplied directly by Munoz.

## Military Service

* Enlisted in the U.S. Army in 1990, initially qualifying as an infantryman (11B).
* Served with the 75th Ranger Regiment and completed Ranger School as a Distinguished Honor Graduate.
* Subsequently qualified as a Special Forces Weapons Sergeant (18B) and Special Forces Intelligence Sergeant (18F).
* Served with the 5th Special Forces Group (Airborne).
* Transitioned into the warrant officer corps, eventually serving as a Special Forces Warrant Officer (180A) and Assistant Detachment Commander.
* Completed Army rotary-wing aviation training, qualifying as a UH-60 Black Hawk pilot.
* Additional assignments included the 101st Airborne Division (Air Assault), 7th Special Forces Group (Airborne), and U.S. Army Special Operations Command.
* Completed five combat tours during his 25-year military career.
* Retired in 2015 as a Chief Warrant Officer Four (CW4).

## ODA 774 — Iraq, 2005

* Deployed to Iraq from February through October 2005 during Operation Iraqi Freedom.
* Assigned to ODA 774, supporting a Special Forces Liaison Element (SFLE).
* Operated from Camp Charlie in Babil Province, south of Baghdad.
* Supported the Salvadoran Task Force operating under Polish command.
* Worked with Salvadoran Comando Especial Anti-Terrorista (CEAT) personnel and Hillah SWAT.
* Responsibilities included coordinating humanitarian assistance during the day and conducting influence activities at night.
* Awarded the Salvadoran Presidential Medal of Gold, presented by the Salvadoran Minister of Defense.

## Four Uniforms, Three Roles

* In correspondence during 2026, Munoz explained that he was issued four DCU blouses in January 2005, shortly before deploying to Iraq.
* Two operational DCUs: Modified with pockets added to the upper sleeves and worn during operations (OPs).
* One FOB DCU: Standard configuration without qualification badges, worn for everyday duties at the Forward Operating Base.
* One garrison DCU: Fully badged configuration reserved for official meetings and engagements with senior personnel.
* The two surviving blouses represent the FOB and garrison configurations.
* The FOB example exhibits considerably more wear than the fully badged blouse.
* These distinctions illustrate how Munoz maintained different uniform configurations for different duties during the same deployment.

## Provenance and Photographic Documentation

* Circa 2007–2008, Munoz sold military equipment at the Bragg Boulevard Flea Market near Fort Bragg, North Carolina.
* Both DCU blouses were acquired directly from Munoz by a military collector during this period.
* The uniforms subsequently entered the present collection through separate transactions in 2026.
* Direct correspondence was established with Munoz following their acquisition.
* Munoz provided two period photographs taken in Iraq during his 2005 deployment, showing him wearing the exact fully badged DCU.
* The photographs confirm the original arrangement of qualification badges and insignia and document the blouse being worn in theater.
* Munoz also provided firsthand information concerning the four DCU configurations, his deployment with ODA 774, and his work with Salvadoran and Iraqi partner forces.
